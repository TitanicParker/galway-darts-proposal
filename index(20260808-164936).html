Chapter 1 · Orientation

The competition in 30 seconds

You belong to a 16-player group. Every Tuesday you play three compulsory matches. Those three matches give you a Tuesday performance profile and monthly points. Your Tuesday performance also helps decide who you play next week. Four Tuesdays create the monthly table, and the monthly table decides who goes up, stays, or goes down.

3 matches→Tuesday result→next opponents+monthly points→group movement
The basic unit

One reusable 16-player group

Everything in the format happens inside groups of exactly 16 players. The same internal system can be repeated for a larger club.

16 playersin one group
4 podsinside the group
24 compulsory matchesper group each Tuesday
Scalability is a consequence of this reusable 16-player unit. The detailed 48 / 64 / 80 / 96 arithmetic comes later.
Your long-term level

Group 1 is strongest

Groups are ordered by playing level. Group 1 is the strongest group; Group 2 is below Group 1; Group 3 is below Group 2; and any additional groups continue in the same direction.

Move uptoward Group 1
Retainstay in your current group
Move downto the next lower group
Your group is your long-term competitive level. Your A–P letter inside that group is temporary.
Chapter 2 · Weekly position

Every group has temporary A–P positions

A group contains 16 positions: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M, N, O, P.

These are not permanent identities and they are not the monthly promotion table. They are the group's weekly A–P positions.

A → P

Your current letter helps determine who you play next.

Who you play

A–P creates four pods

The 16 positions are always split in the same pattern:

Pod 1A, E, I, M
Pod 2B, F, J, N
Pod 3C, G, K, O
Pod 4D, H, L, P
Each pod takes one player from each quarter of the current A–P order.
The compulsory darts

You play everyone in your pod

4 playersper pod
3 matchesper player
6 matchesper pod
24 matchesper group

Every compulsory match is first to two legs. A match must therefore finish 2–0 or 2–1 to one player.

Exact weekly re-sort rule

Tonight re-sorts A–P for next Tuesday

  1. After the 24 compulsory matches, put all 16 players together.
  2. Sort them by that night's S-profile: S1 first, then S2, through to S20.
  3. If two or more players have the same S-profile, they keep the same relative order in which they entered that Tuesday.
Performance determines movement. Equal performance protects the position already earned.

Only the compulsory pod matches create the weekly S-profile used in this re-sort.

The weekly feedback loop

New A–P means new opponents

If you move from M to F, you do not carry the M fixtures with you. Next Tuesday you are simply F, so you enter the B–F–J–N pod.

Tuesday results→S-profile→new A–P→new pod→3 new fixtures

This is why A–P matters: it is the short-term sorting state that generates your next set of opponents.

Chapter 5 · What am I trying to achieve?

Three levels of objective

TuesdayEarn the strongest S-profile possible.
MonthAccumulate enough S-points to finish safely or earn promotion.
SeasonReach the strongest appropriate group and stay there.

A–P is tactically important because it determines your next opponents. Your monthly S-points and group status are the larger competitive objectives.

Simple player strategy

What should I try to do?

  1. Win the match. A match win moves you into a stronger S-profile band.
  2. Prefer 2–0 to 2–1. Clean wins strengthen the profile.
  3. If losing, fight for the leg. A 1–2 loss is better than 0–2.
  4. Do it across all four Tuesdays. Monthly performance rewards consistency as well as peaks.
  5. Turn up. A full-night absence gives the lowest profile, S20.
Month-end consequence

Promoted, retained or relegated

The monthly S-points table orders the 16 players in each group for the month.

Top 2Move up one group, except players already in Group 1.
Middle playersRemain in the same group.
Bottom 2Move down one group, except players already in the lowest group.
At every boundary, two players travel each way: for example, bottom two of Group 1 ↔ top two of Group 2.
Start the next month cleanly

Then A–P resets alphabetically

  1. The monthly table closes.
  2. Promotion and relegation create the new group memberships.
  3. Inside each new 16-player group, names are placed A–P alphabetically.
  4. The next four-Tuesday cycle begins.
Group membership carries over. The old A–P letters do not.
Core player rule

What happens if I miss Tuesday?

  • A fully absent player receives three scheduled 0–2 defeats.
  • That produces S20 for the night.
  • There are no rearranged or postponed compulsory matches.
  • If a player starts the night and later withdraws, completed results stand; unplayed scheduled matches become 0–2 defeats.

The rare case where both players scheduled against one another are absent still requires a final printed administrative convention.

Chapter 6 · For organisers

Every compulsory fixture is known in advance

Once the current A–P positions are published, each player's pod is known. Therefore every compulsory match for the next Tuesday can be listed before the night begins.

A–P known→pods known→fixtures known→fixture manifest

The manifest fixes who must play whom. It does not need to fix the exact board or exact time.

Live dispatch

The MC manages traffic, not rules

  • Call the next eligible fixture whenever a board becomes free.
  • Do not call a player who is already playing or chalking.
  • Record every completed result.
  • Keep boards occupied and move spare capacity to the group falling behind.
  • The competition rules have already decided the opponents.
Flexible execution

Boards are flexible. Fixtures are fixed.

Pod fixtures may be played in any sensible order and on any available board. There is no mathematical need to finish Pod 1 before Pod 2, or to keep one pod permanently on one board.

Chalking convention: the loser of the previous game on a board chalks the next game on that board, unless the MC needs that player immediately for another match.
The Tuesday-night loop

By the end of the night, next Tuesday can already exist

results → S-profiles → weekly re-sort A–P → new pods → next Tuesday's fixture manifest

The draw for the following week is not improvised later. It emerges deterministically from the completed Tuesday.

Chapter 8 · Why these choices?

Why separate weekly A–P from monthly S-points?

The original idea allowed the final Tuesday's A–P state to carry too much influence over promotion and relegation. Accumulating four S-point results makes all four Tuesdays contribute to the monthly decision.

79.3%pairwise agreement with the raw 12-match benchmark using only Week-4 A–P
98.4%using simple ordinal S-points
99.4%using the proposed tier-weighted S-points

These figures come from simulation modelling, not historical club data.

Validation · Monthly reset

Why is alphabetical A–P acceptable?

Alphabetical seeding creates a small transient Week-1 ordering effect because equal S-profiles preserve incoming order. The modelling found that effect largely washed out by Week 2.

In the equal-ability stress test, mean monthly rank varied by only about 0.19 of a place across initial A–P positions.

So alphabetical order is transparent, repeatable and sufficiently neutral for the monthly reset.

Validation · Group movement

Why two up / two down?

Simulation of eight-month seasons found that two movers per group boundary gave the best tested balance between allowing strong players to climb and avoiding excessive month-to-month churn.

Technical validation

The pod is mathematically closed

Four players create six compulsory matches. Each match has four possible scored outcomes from a labelled perspective. Therefore a complete pod has:

4⁶ = 4,096
  • 3,680 configurations produce one unique first-place player.
  • 384 produce exactly two players tied for first.
  • 32 produce exactly three players tied for first.
  • A four-way tie for first is impossible.

This is technical evidence that the pod outcome space can be exhaustively anticipated; a player does not need it in order to play.

The whole system

One causal chain

Group→A–P→Pod→3 matches→S-profile→weekly re-sort
S-profile→S-points→4 Tuesdays→monthly table→promotion / retention / relegation

Then the new groups are formed, A–P resets alphabetically, and the same process begins again.

Scaling does not change the core rules. It simply repeats the same 16-player group as many times as the venue can practically support.
