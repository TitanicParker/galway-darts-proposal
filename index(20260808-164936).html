Darts Club Format — Working Explainer

Chapter 1 · The idea

One format. Any size.

The club does not need a different ruleset for 48, 64, 80 or 96 players. It needs one 16-player competition engine repeated in parallel.

Core principle: scale by adding another group of 16 — not by changing the internal rules.

Chapter 2 · The 16-player engine

Every group uses A to P.

Each group always contains 16 positions. Those positions are temporary sorting positions, not permanent player identities.

The pod

Three matches per player.

4 playersper pod
3 matchesper player
6 matchesper pod
24 matchesper 16-player group

Every match is first to two legs. A match can finish 2–0 or 2–1.

The weekly ladder

A–P answers one question:

Who do I play next?

The weekly A–P ladder is a sorting and fixture-generation device. It is not, by itself, the monthly promotion table.

Chapter 3 · Start of each month

Reset the letters. Keep the groups.

  1. Promotion and relegation produce the new membership of each group.
  2. Each group contains 16 players.
  3. The 16 names are placed A–P alphabetically.
  4. The new four-Tuesday sorting cycle begins.
Group membership is the long-term memory. Letter position is temporary.

The MC

The MC manages traffic, not rules.

  • Call the next eligible match when a board becomes free.
  • Avoid calling a player who is already playing or chalking.
  • Record completed results.
  • Keep boards occupied.
  • Use freed capacity where it is needed most.
Flexible board flow

The match order does not matter.

Pod matches can be played in any order and on any suitable board. The fixture itself is fixed; the board and sequence are operational choices.

Standard chalking rule: the loser of the previous match chalks the next match on that board, unless the MC needs that player immediately for another fixture.
By the end of the night

By 11 p.m., next Tuesday can already be set.

completed results → S-profiles → new A–P order → new pods → next Tuesday's manifest

The next draw is generated by the competition rules. It does not need to be invented during the week.

Chapter 5 · The player profile

One Tuesday = one S-profile.

Each player has three compulsory matches. Each match produces one of four individual results:

2–0win
2–1win
1–2loss
0–2loss

Across three matches these collapse into 20 aggregate S-profiles: S1 to S20.

What am I actually trying to do?

Three levels of objective.

Tuesday Earn the strongest S-profile possible.
Month Build enough S-points to finish safely or earn promotion.
Season Reach the strongest appropriate group and stay there.
Chapter 7 · Player strategy

How should a player try to win?

  1. Win the match. Match wins dominate the S-profile bands.
  2. Win 2–0 if possible. Clean wins improve the profile.
  3. If losing, fight for the leg. A 1–2 loss is better than 0–2.
  4. Be consistent across four Tuesdays. One great night does not erase three poor nights.
  5. Turn up. An absence produces three 0–2 defeats and S20.
The ladder psychology

Protect your group. Improve your position.

You should care about moving upward in A–P because it changes who you play next, but your larger objective is monthly S-performance and ultimately your group status.

Chapter 8 · Promotion & relegation

The monthly table moves players between groups.

PromotedMove up one group.
RetainedStay in the same group.
RelegatedMove down one group.

Current statistical recommendation: two up / two down at each boundary.

The hierarchy of memory

Three different things remember three different timescales.

GroupSeason-level status
S-pointsMonthly performance
A–PWeekly sorting state
Absence rule

Missing a Tuesday has a fixed consequence.

  • Each unplayed scheduled match becomes a 0–2 defeat.
  • A fully absent player therefore records S20 for the night.
  • No rearranged matches.
  • Completed matches stand if a player withdraws mid-night.
Chapter 9 · What the modelling says

Why separate A–P from monthly S-points?

79.3%pairwise agreement when using only the Week-4 ladder
98.4%with simple S20→1 ordinal points
99.4%with tier-weighted S-points

These are simulation results from the statistical review, not observed historical league data.

Alphabetical reset

Does starting A–P alphabetically matter?

The modelling found only a small Week-1 effect. By Week 2 it was tiny, and the monthly ranking varied by only about 0.19 of a place across starting positions in the equal-ability stress test.

Conclusion: alphabetical monthly reseeding is simple, transparent and acceptably neutral.
Movement rate

Why two up / two down?

In the eight-month simulations, two movers per boundary gave the best long-run balance between sorting ability and avoiding excessive churn.

The pod is mathematically closed

Every pod outcome can be anticipated.

Four players create six compulsory matches. Each match has four possible scored outcomes, so a complete pod has:

4⁶ = 4,096

possible labelled score configurations.

  • 3,680 produce one unique first-place player.
  • 384 produce exactly two tied for first.
  • 32 produce exactly three tied for first.
  • A four-way tie for first is impossible.

Summary

The format stays uniform.

Sixteen-player groups are the reusable unit. The weekly ladder decides opponents. S-profiles measure the night. Four S-scores build the monthly table. Monthly movement changes group membership. Scaling adds more copies of the same engine.
